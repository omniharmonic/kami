# A living pocket atlas

The world carries the personality. Its controls should be quiet, readable glass over a physical place, with a large original seed creature as the focus of a visit. Mint canopy `#cbe9d7`, lagoon `#83d6ca`, petal pink `#efb6c9`, milk `#fff8e8`, and deep leaf `#37685b` connect the landscape with the console. The existing product typefaces remain in controls; no additional font download.

The regional view now uses an actual elevation grid, not the earlier sine-wave landscape. `scripts/build-terrain.py` downloads four public Terrarium tiles at zoom 9 and samples a 193×193 grid. The bundled asset is approximately 190 KB uncompressed and does not require a tile API key or external requests at runtime. Source: [Mapzen Terrain Tiles on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), with [Tilezen attribution and licensing](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md). United States 3DEP and global GMTED2010/SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. These US elevation data are public domain; the source conditions remain linked in the asset. Generated September 7, 2026. Elevations are historical terrain, not current sensor observations.

Coverage is the Boulder-centered Front Range: 105.8°W–104.95°W, 39.65°N–40.35°N. Samples are rounded to the nearest metre after nearest-pixel sampling of the public DEM, with bilinear rendering between samples. Grid spacing is roughly 380–410 m; this is regional relief, not survey-grade site topography. The 150×162 scene footprint approximates the geographic aspect ratio at 40°N. Elevation has 2.5× visual exaggeration, with 1 scene unit approximately 480 ground metres. Vertical accuracy is not independently validated. No fabricated stream network is overlaid on this DEM.

The twin's `web/src/layers/terrain.ts` establishes the important approach: marker elevation follows the rendered ground, rather than zero. Our Three.js renderer uses the same principle with the sampled DEM height. `WorldBeing` accepts optional public nonsensitive `longitude` / `latitude`; callers must supply verified locations. Existing x/y or default placements remain scenic and are not geographic claims. Out-of-coverage locations are not asserted to be accurately positioned.

On a visit, a sculpted habitat vignette appears: moss island, smooth stones, flowers and an illustrative pond for water beings. These details, including all vegetation, are art, not field inventories or assertions of flow. A physical 3D original seed sprite has ears, leaves, tiny feet, cheeks and a field satchel. Kind selects a leaf, water, alpine or animal palette. Sleep is kept distinct from distress. Body motion is breathing/idle animation; it does not represent backend actions being executed. The current product mood selects open or sleeping eyes without inventing readings.

Performance: shared instanced grove geometry, device pixel ratio capped at 1.7, approximately 30 fps, no rendering while the document is hidden. Reduced-motion users get still camera transitions and still sprite poses. WebGL failure keeps the SVG scene and accessible buttons; failed/corrupt DEM loading is labelled illustrated instead of silently claiming real terrain. Geographic renderer assets must never be imported into model tool output.

Verification: terrain tests cover orientation/scaling and reject incomplete or nonfinite data; web typecheck passed after implementation. The orchestrator inspected the production world and habitat in desktop and narrow browser windows. Production GPU benchmarks and the broader device matrix remain unmeasured. The asset's topography does not turn the miniature habitat into a site-accurate reconstruction.
